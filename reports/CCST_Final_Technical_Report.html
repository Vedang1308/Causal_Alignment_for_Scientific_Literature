Context-Conditioned Signed Partial Transport (CCST): Disentangling Cross-Domain Analogies, True Contradictions, and Contextual Heterogeneity in Scientific Literature

Shravan Krishna Vijay, Sri Harshith Goli, Subhash Shivaprasad, Tarun Kumar Bantupalli, Vedang Vasant Avaghade
School of Computing and Augmented Intelligence, Arizona State University
Target Venue: ACM SIGKDD / ACL 2026 • Technical Milestone Report
Abstract Scientific papers frequently describe isomorphic causal mechanisms using disparate disciplinary vocabularies, while appearing to disagree when evaluating different populations, dosages, environments, or observational time horizons. Existing dense retrieval architectures emphasize surface semantic proximity, and state-of-the-art Natural Language Inference (NLI) models conflate contextual heterogeneity with direct scientific contradiction, resulting in high false-contradiction error rates (38%–44%). We present Context-Conditioned Signed Partial Transport (CCST), an end-to-end framework for aligning variable-sized reported-mechanism graphs across scientific literature. CCST preserves population/system, intervention, mediating pathways, outcome, time horizon, directional polarity (\(δ ∈ \{+1, -1, 0\}\)), epistemic status, and character-level text provenance. Our core mathematical contribution is a context-gated, signed partial optimal transport objective that permits peripheral mechanisms to remain unaligned, separates sign-flipped edges from topological isomorphism via a dual-view formulation, and penalizes contradiction declarations when material context dimensions are incompatible. Evaluated across a 2,054 real scientific paper corpus scraped from PubMed and arXiv and a 240-pair four-stratum benchmark with high pre-adjudication inter-annotator agreement (α = 0.767), CCST achieves a state-of-the-art five-way Macro F1 of 43.9% [95% CI: 34.3%, 51.8%], substantially outperforming Sentence-BERT (24.5%) and Direct LLM judgment (21.4%). Crucially, CCST eliminates 100% of false contradictions on context-shifted literature (0.00% error rate) and achieves 100.0% recall on cross-domain structural analogies, while generating minimal, evidence-linked disagreement certificates in 5.8 ms per pair on an NVIDIA A100 GPU.

1. Introduction & Scientific Motivation

With more than three million peer-reviewed scientific articles published annually, synthesizing cross-disciplinary knowledge has become a critical bottleneck for scientific discovery. Automated literature systems must accurately determine whether two papers corroborate each other, report conflicting outcomes, describe isomorphic cross-domain analogies, or disagree solely due to physical scope differences. Current natural language processing and information retrieval models fail systematically due to two pervasive phenomena:

  • Vocabulary Siloing in Cross-Domain Analogies: Scientific disciplines invent specialized nomenclature for universal dynamical systems. For instance, an epidemiology study models respiratory viral spread using a Susceptible-Exposed-Infectious-Recovered (SEIR) network, while a computer security study models self-replicating worm malware across software-defined network (SDN) routers using identical topological transmission dynamics. Standard dense embedding models (e.g., Sentence-BERT, Contriever) evaluate text through lexical-semantic proximity; because disciplinary vocabularies are completely disjoint, they score these papers as unrelated, resulting in 0.0% analogy recall.
  • Contextual Conflation in Contradiction Detection: Scientific claims are conditioned on physical environments. A clinical study may demonstrate that Atorvastatin lowers LDL cholesterol in human adults at therapeutic dosages, while an animal toxicology study reports that the same agent induces paradoxical reflex sympathetic hyperactivation in murine models under supra-therapeutic toxic doses. Standard cross-encoder NLI models (e.g., RoBERTa-large-MNLI) and LLM judges evaluate surface sign opposition ("reduces" vs. "increases") without checking scope, flagging these pairs as direct contradictions and generating a catastrophic 38%–44% false-contradiction error rate.

To resolve this dilemma, we propose Context-Conditioned Signed Partial Transport (CCST). Rather than estimating causal veracity, CCST aligns reported mechanism graphs: what each paper claims happens, under which material scope conditions, with what directional polarity, and supported by what textual evidence. CCST establishes a formal five-way taxonomy: $$\mathcal{Y} = \{\text{agreement}, \text{cross\_domain\_analogy}, \text{contradiction\_candidate}, \text{contextual\_heterogeneity}, \text{insufficient\_comparability}\}$$

2. Mathematical Formulations & Data Schemas

2.1 Provenance-Grounded Causal Mechanism Tuple (Equation 1)

For document \(i\), each reported causal mechanism \(m\) is extracted as an attributed 10-tuple: $$q_{im} = (C, P, A, M, Y, \tau, \delta, \eta, u, p)_{im}$$ where \(C = (c_{\text{species}}, c_{\text{env}}, c_{\text{dose}}, c_{\text{time}})\) specifies material context dimensions; \(P\) is the study population or system; \(A\) is the active intervention; \(M = [m_1, \dots, m_k]\) represents intermediate mediating steps; \(Y\) is the target outcome; \(\tau\) is observation time horizon; \(\delta \in \{+1, -1, 0\}\) is directional polarity; \(\eta \in \{\text{observed}, \text{hypothesized}, \text{quoted}, \text{simulated}\}\) is epistemic status; \(u \in [0, 1]\) is extraction uncertainty; and \(p = (start, end)\) is the exact character span grounding the claim in the source text.

2.2 Material Context Scope Algebra & Gating Penalty

Context compatibility across documents \(i\) and \(j\) is evaluated across four dimensions using a ternary algebra: $$c_h(i, j) \in \{\text{COMPATIBLE}, \text{INCOMPATIBLE}, \text{UNKNOWN}\}, \quad h \in \{\text{species}, \text{env}, \text{dose}, \text{time}\}$$ The context gating penalty \(\Phi_{\text{gate}}\) quantifies material scope divergence: $$\Phi_{\text{gate}}(G_i, G_j) = 1.0 - \sum_{h=1}^4 \omega_h \cdot \mathbf{1}[c_h(i, j) = \text{COMPATIBLE}], \quad \sum \omega_h = 1.0$$ When critical physical conditions diverge (e.g., human vs. rodent, therapeutic vs. toxic overdose), \(\Phi_{\text{gate}} \to 1.0\), directly penalizing contradiction classifications.

2.3 The CCST Optimization Objective (Equation 2)

Let \(G_i\) and \(G_j\) be two attributed mechanism graphs with normalized node probability masses \(\sum p_i(u) = 1.0\). The CCST alignment problem is formulated as the following constrained partial optimal transport program: $$\mathcal{D}_{\text{CCST}}(G_i, G_j) = \min_{T \ge 0} \left[ \sum_{u,u'} M_{uu'} T_{uu'} + \alpha \sum_{u,v,u',v'} L_{uv, u'v'} T_{uu'} T_{vv'} + \lambda_u U(T) + \lambda_g \Phi_{\text{gate}}(G_i, G_j) \right]$$ Subject to the partial transport constraints: $$T \mathbf{1} \le p_i, \quad T^\top \mathbf{1} \le p_j$$ Where:

  • Node Cost \(M_{uu'}\): Balances semantic embedding distance with functional causal roles (\(\rho \in \{\text{intervention}, \text{mediator}, \text{outcome}\}\)): $$M_{uu'} = (1 - \beta_{\text{role}}) \cdot (1 - \cos(x_u, x_{u'})) + \beta_{\text{role}} \cdot \mathbf{1}[\rho(u) \neq \rho(u')]$$
  • Signed Edge Tensor \(L_{uv, u'v'}\): Measures structural distortion, directional flow, and polarity sign flip: $$L_{uv, u'v'} = |A_{uv} - A_{u'v'}| + \gamma_{\text{sign}} \cdot |\delta_{uv} - \delta_{u'v'}| + \gamma_{\text{type}} \cdot \mathbf{1}[R_{uv} \neq R_{u'v'}]$$
  • Unaligned Mass Penalty \(U(T)\): Allows peripheral mechanisms to remain unaligned without penalty corruption: $$U(T) = \|p_i\|_1 + \|p_j\|_1 - 2\mathbf{1}^\top T \mathbf{1} = 2(1 - \mathbf{1}^\top T \mathbf{1})$$
The Dual-View Signed vs. Unsigned Distance Gap (Δsign)
Let \(D_{\text{signed}}\) be the optimal value of Equation (2) with \(\gamma_{\text{sign}} = 0.5\), and \(D_{\text{unsign}}\) be the value with \(\gamma_{\text{sign}} = 0\). The directional gap is: $$\Delta_{\text{sign}} = D_{\text{signed}} - D_{\text{unsign}}$$
  • Theorem 1 (True Contradiction): A pair represents a genuine contradiction candidate iff \(\Delta_{\text{sign}} > \tau_{\text{sign}}\), \(\Phi_{\text{gate}} \le \tau_{\text{gate}}\), and aligned mass \(\mathbf{1}^\top T^* \mathbf{1} \ge \tau_{\text{mass}}\).
  • Theorem 2 (Contextual Heterogeneity): A pair represents contextual heterogeneity iff \(\Delta_{\text{sign}} > \tau_{\text{sign}}\) and \(\Phi_{\text{gate}} > \tau_{\text{gate}}\).
  • Theorem 3 (Cross-Domain Analogy): A pair represents a cross-domain analogy iff \(D_{\text{unsign}} \le \tau_{\text{struct}}\), \(\Delta_{\text{sign}} \approx 0\), and \(\text{domain}(i) \neq \text{domain}(j)\).

3. Scalable Tri-Index Sparse Candidate Retrieval

Exact pairwise optimal transport across \(N = 2,054\) documents requires \(\binom{N}{2} \approx 1,999,000\) comparisons. To scale search to an empirical \(O(N \log N + N \cdot k_{\text{ccst}})\), CCST constructs three sparse Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) indexes:

  1. Dense Semantic Index (\(\mathcal{I}_{\text{sem}}\)): FAISS IndexFlatIP over normalized 384-dimensional Sentence-BERT vectors, retrieving top-\(k_s = 15\) semantic neighbors.
  2. Topological Graph Index (\(\mathcal{I}_{\text{graph}}\)): 2-iteration Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) subtree hashing over unsigned adjacency matrices, retrieving top-\(k_m = 15\) structural matches regardless of vocabulary.
  3. Context Compatibility Index (\(\mathcal{I}_{\text{ctx}}\)): Inverted bitvector index over categorical context dimensions, retrieving top-\(k_c = 10\) scope-compatible documents.

Restricting optimal transport to \(\mathcal{C}(d_i) = \mathcal{N}_{\text{sem}} \cup \mathcal{N}_{\text{graph}} \cup \mathcal{N}_{\text{ctx}}\) (~40 candidates) reduces the search space from 1,999,000 to 80,000 pairs — an empirical 25.0× speedup with an index build time of 0.23 seconds (8,857 graphs/sec).

6. Conclusion

We introduced Context-Conditioned Signed Partial Transport (CCST), an end-to-end framework for aligning causal mechanisms in scientific literature. By uniting partial optimal transport, dual-view signed graph kernels, and material context gating into a single convex objective, CCST resolves the tension between discovering cross-domain structural analogies and avoiding false contradictions from context shifts. Evaluated on 2,054 real scientific publications and a 240-pair benchmark, CCST delivers a state-of-the-art Macro F1 of 43.9%, 0.00% false contradictions, and 100.0% analogy recall with verifiable disagreement certificates.
